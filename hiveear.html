How HiveEar listens

A colony's sound changes before its behaviour does. Beekeepers with decades of experience can hear a queenless hive. HiveEar measures the same signals so that any beekeeper can.

Step 1

Record

A phone held to the hive wall for 30 s, or a $15 sensor inside the hive that records every hour.

Step 2

Spectrum

Short-time Fourier transform: 2048-point frames, 0–2 kHz, about 4 Hz resolution. The steady hum is separated from short bursts.

Step 3

Five signals

Hum pitch, spectral flatness, high/low band ratio, loudness swings and queen-piping events per minute.

Step 4

Verdict and plan

Probabilities for three colony states, a 0–100 health score, and what to do in the next 48 hours.

Signals HiveEar reads

Frequency ranges come from published bee acoustics research.

Colony stateWhat it sounds likeMeasured asWhy it matters
QueenrightSteady hum, fundamental 200–280 Hz, clean harmonicsLow flatness, low band ratioBaseline. Check again in a week.
QueenlessRaised, rough "roar" with uneven loudnessHum pitch above 280 Hz, flatness above 0.3, band ratio above 1Without a queen the colony dies within weeks. A new queen must arrive in time.
Pre-swarmQueen "tooting" at 400–500 Hz, answered by "quacking" at 300–350 HzNarrow tonal bursts per minuteHalf the bees leave with the old queen, and the season's honey goes with them.

Where the model is today

MVP 0.3

An explainable rule model on five acoustic features with thresholds taken from the research literature. Every verdict shows the numbers behind it. It runs fully in the browser, so no audio leaves the phone.
